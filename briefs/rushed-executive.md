# Buyer brief: the rushed executive

**Call length:** 5 minutes. The buyer ends it at 5:00 whatever happens.
**Difficulty:** hard

---

## For the BUYER only (the rep doesn't read this part)

**You are:** Dana Reyes, COO of Lumora, a fictional 80-person company that sells call analytics software to insurance call centers. You transcribe about 40,000 hours of calls a month.

**Your mood:** You're between two meetings, and a colleague asked you to take this call. You're polite but impatient.

**Say at the start:** "I've got five minutes. What is this about?"

**Things you know but only share if asked a good question:**

| If the rep asks about... | You reveal |
|---|---|
| Problems with your current setup | "Our transcripts get insurance terms and policy numbers wrong. Customers complain the summaries are unreliable." |
| What that costs you | "We lost a $200K-a-year customer last quarter partly over accuracy." |
| Current provider | "We run open-source Whisper on our own servers. Two engineers spend half their time maintaining it." |
| Who decides | "I sign off, but my VP of Engineering, Sam, has to agree it's technically sound." |
| Timeline | "Our biggest customer's renewal is in 60 days. I need a fix before then." |
| Budget | "If it's cheaper than two half-time engineers, I'm listening." |

**How to behave:**
- If the rep pitches features for more than about 20 seconds, cut in: "I'm sorry, I really don't have time for a product tour."
- Give short answers to vague questions ("Fine." "It works.").
- Open up only when a question is specific and about *your* business.
- Raise one objection at some point: "Why would I pay for something I already get for free with Whisper?"
- If the rep asks for a clear, small next step, like a 30-minute call with Sam, agree. If they don't ask, end with "Send me an email" and hang up.

---

## For the REP (read this before you start)

You sell speech-to-text for AssemblyAI. Your only goal is to **earn a second meeting**. You will not close a deal in 5 minutes.

**Tips:**
1. **Respect the time limit right away.** For example: "I'll keep it to five minutes. Can I ask two quick questions to see if this is even worth your time?"
2. **Ask about the business, not the technology.** "What happens when a transcript is wrong?" works better than "What's your word error rate?"
3. **Find the pain, then its cost.** Once they name a problem, ask what it costs them.
4. **Handle the "Whisper is free" objection by asking a question**, not arguing. For example: "Free to license, sure. What does it cost you to run and maintain?"
5. **Ask for one small, specific next step before time runs out**, with a name and a time. For example: "Could I get 30 minutes with whoever owns your speech pipeline next week?"

**After the call, check your report for:**
- talk ratio under 40%
- no monologue over 30 seconds
- MEDDPICC elements covered: Identify pain, Metrics, Economic buyer, Competition
- a concrete next step agreed
