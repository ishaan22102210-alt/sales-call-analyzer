# Follow-up email

Send it **the same day**, ideally within an hour. Keep it under 150 words. The buyer should be able to forward it to their boss without editing.

## Template

> **Subject:** [Their company] + AssemblyAI: next steps
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for the time today. Here's what I heard, so you can correct me if I got anything wrong:
>
> - **The problem:** [in their words, e.g. "transcripts get policy numbers wrong, so summaries aren't reliable"]
> - **What it costs:** [e.g. "a $200K customer lost last quarter, and two engineers at half capacity"]
> - **Why now:** [e.g. "your biggest customer renews in two months"]
>
> **Agreed next step:** [e.g. "a 30-minute call with Sam on Tuesday at 2pm to plan a test on 50 of your calls"]
>
> I'll send a calendar invite. Is there anyone else who should join?
>
> [Your name]

## Why this works
- **Their words, not yours.** It shows you listened, and it's easy to forward.
- **The cost is in writing.** That helps them make the case internally.
- **One clear next step** with a date. Not "let me know if you have questions".
- **"Correct me"** invites a reply, which keeps the conversation going.

## If no next step was agreed
Replace the "Agreed next step" line with:
> "Would it be useful to test it on 20 of your hardest recordings? If so, I can have results to you by Friday."

## If they don't reply
- **After 3 days:** "Hi [Name], just bumping this up. Does Tuesday still work with Sam?"
- **After 7 days:** share something useful (an article, or a relevant example), not "just checking in".
- **After 14 days:** "Should I close this out for now? No problem if the timing's wrong." _(This "break-up email" often gets the most replies.)_
