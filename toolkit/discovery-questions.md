# Discovery questions

**The rule:** ask, then listen. Ask about their **business**, not about the technology. After each answer, dig one level deeper with "Tell me more", "What happens then?" or "Why does that matter?"

Each section maps to a letter of MEDDPICC, so your analyzer score goes up when you use them.

---

## Open the call (earn permission)
- "I'll keep it short. Can I ask a couple of quick questions to see if this is worth your time?"
- "What made you take this call?" _(Often their real reason comes out right here.)_

## Pain: what's broken? (I = Identify pain)
- "How are you turning audio into text today?"
- "What's working well about that? And what isn't?"
- "When a transcript is wrong, what happens next? Who notices?"
- "Can you give me a recent example?"
- "If you could fix one thing about it tomorrow, what would it be?"

## Cost: what does the pain cost? (M = Metrics)
- "What does that cost you: in money, time, or customers?"
- "How many hours of audio do you process a month?"
- "How many engineers spend time maintaining it?"
- "What's your current cost per hour of audio, or per call?"
- "If this got fixed, what would change for the business?"

## Decision: who decides and how? (E = Economic buyer, D = Decision process)
- "Besides you, who else would be involved in a decision like this?"
- "Who has to say yes to the budget?"
- "Last time you brought in a new tool, how did that go? What were the steps?"
- "Is there a security or legal review?" _(This is the P = Paper process.)_

## Criteria: what does "good" look like? (D = Decision criteria)
- "If you tested a new provider, how would you judge it?"
- "What matters most: accuracy, speed, price, or something else?"
- "What number would it need to hit? For example, a reply in under X milliseconds."

## Competition: what else are they considering? (C = Competition)
- "What else have you looked at?"
- "What do you like about your current provider?" _(Never criticise the competitor. Let them say what's missing.)_
- "What would happen if you did nothing?" _(Doing nothing is usually the biggest competitor.)_

## Timing: why now?
- "Is there a deadline or event that makes this urgent?"
- "What happens if this isn't solved in three months?"

## Champion: who wants this to happen? (C = Champion)
- "Who on your team feels this problem most?"
- "If we ran a test, who would own it on your side?"

## Close the call (always end with a next step)
- "Based on what you've told me, it sounds like [pain] is costing you [cost]. Is that fair?" _(Summarise before asking.)_
- "Would it make sense to run a quick test on your own audio and compare?"
- "Could we get 30 minutes with [technical person] next week? Does Tuesday or Thursday work?" _(Give two options, never "let me know when".)_

---

## Avoid these
| Instead of... | Ask... |
|---|---|
| "Are you happy with your current solution?" (they'll say yes) | "What would you change about it?" |
| "Do you care about accuracy?" (everyone says yes) | "What happens when a transcript is wrong?" |
| "Can I show you a demo?" (too early) | "Can I ask what you'd need to see?" |
| Three questions in one breath | One question, then silence |
