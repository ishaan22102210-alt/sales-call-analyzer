# Objection handling

An objection is not a "no". It's the buyer telling you what they're worried about.

## The 4-step method: LAER
1. **Listen.** Let them finish. Don't jump in.
2. **Acknowledge.** "That's fair." "Makes sense." (Agree with the feeling, not necessarily the claim.)
3. **Explore.** Ask a question to understand what's behind it.
4. **Respond.** Only now give your answer, and keep it short.

Most beginners skip straight to step 4 and argue. Step 3 is where the sale happens.

---

## Common objections when selling speech AI

### "Whisper is free. Why would I pay?"
- **Acknowledge:** "Fair. Whisper is a great model, and free to download."
- **Explore:** "What does it take to run it? Servers, engineers' time, keeping it up to date?" Then: "How does it do on your audio, like accents, background noise and industry terms?"
- **Respond:** "Usually the model is free but running it isn't. Teams compare the full cost: servers plus engineer time. Want to work that out together?" → use [roi_calculator.py](roi_calculator.py)

### "We already use Deepgram (or another provider)."
- **Acknowledge:** "Makes sense. They're a solid company."
- **Explore:** "What made you pick them? If you could change one thing, what would it be?"
- **Respond:** "Most teams don't switch on a pitch. They test. Would it be worth running the same 50 calls through both and comparing the results on your own audio?"
- **Never** criticise the competitor. A fair test makes the argument for you.

### "It's too expensive."
- **Acknowledge:** "Understood. Cost matters at your volume."
- **Explore:** "Expensive compared to what? What are you paying today, including engineer time?"
- **Respond:** compare against the *total* cost of the problem: lost customers, engineer hours, human fixes. Not just the price per hour.

### "Just send me some information."
_This usually means "go away politely"._
- **Acknowledge:** "Happy to."
- **Explore:** "So I send the right thing, which matters more to you: accuracy, speed, or cost?"
- **Respond:** "I'll send that today. Could we grab 15 minutes Thursday to go over whether it fits?" _(Always attach a next step.)_

### "We're not looking right now."
- **Explore:** "Totally fine. Out of curiosity, when you review your tools, what would make you look?"
- **Respond:** "Would it be okay if I checked back in [their timeframe]?" Then actually do it.

### "Is our data safe? We handle sensitive calls."
- **Acknowledge:** "Absolutely the right question, especially in [their industry]."
- **Explore:** "What does your security team usually need to see?"
- **Respond:** point them to AssemblyAI's security and compliance pages, and offer to connect them with the right person. _Never guess at compliance answers. Say "I'll confirm that exactly" and follow up._

### "We'd rather build it ourselves."
- **Explore:** "What's driving that: cost, control, or something else?"
- **Respond:** "That makes sense if speech is your core product. If it isn't, is that where you want your engineers' time going?"

### "Your accuracy can't be that much better."
- **Acknowledge:** "Healthy scepticism. Everyone claims to be the most accurate."
- **Respond:** "So don't take my word for it. Send me 20 of your hardest recordings, and we'll compare."
